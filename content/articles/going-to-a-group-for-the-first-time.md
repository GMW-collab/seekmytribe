---
title: Going to a group for the first time
summary: Practical ways to make the first visit easier, from someone who has watched a lot of first visits.
audience: participants
published: 2026-10-22
---

Walking into a room of people you don't know is hard. That's true for confident
people too; they've just had more practice. A few small things make it easier.

## Pick something with a job to do

Activities with a shared task — a walk, a meal to cook, a puzzle, a garden bed
to dig — take the pressure off conversation. You can talk about the thing in
front of you, or not talk at all.

## Ask before you go

It's normal to want to know what you're walking into. Who will be there? Is
there step-free access? What happens if you arrive late? Send a message to the
organiser. Good organisers like being asked, because it means you're coming.

## Arrive a little early

The first five minutes are usually the quietest. Arriving early means you meet
people one or two at a time instead of walking into a full room.

## Tell the host it's your first time

A host's job is to welcome people. Saying "this is my first time" gives them
permission to introduce you, show you where things are and check in later.

## Have an exit plan

Knowing you can leave makes it easier to stay. Decide in advance that you'll
give it half an hour. If you want to go after that, go. You've still done the
hard part.

## Give it more than one try

Groups feel different on different days. The second visit is almost always
easier than the first, because someone will recognise you.
