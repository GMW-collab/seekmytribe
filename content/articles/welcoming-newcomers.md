---
title: How to make a group welcoming for newcomers
summary: A short guide for hosts and regulars on the small habits that decide whether someone comes back.
audience: hosts
published: 2026-10-22
---

Most people decide whether to return to a group within the first ten minutes.
What happens in those minutes is rarely about the activity itself. It's about
whether anyone noticed them.

## Have someone whose job is to greet

Name one person per session as the greeter. Their job is to watch the door, say
hello, learn the newcomer's name and introduce them to one other person. It
sounds formal. It works because it doesn't rely on anyone remembering.

## Introduce people with a hook

"This is Sam" ends a conversation. "This is Sam, who's just moved here from
Leeds" starts one. Give people something to ask about.

## Watch the circle

Groups of people standing and talking tend to close into tight circles. Leave a
gap. When someone hovers nearby, step back and let them in.

## Explain the unwritten rules

Every group has them: where coats go, whether you pay at the start or end,
whether people stay for a drink afterwards. Say them out loud. Newcomers
shouldn't have to guess.

## Follow up once

If the group has a way of keeping in touch, a short "good to meet you, hope to
see you next time" after a first visit makes a real difference. Once is enough.

## Ask newcomers what would help

People who have just joined can see things regulars can't. Ask them, after a
few visits, what nearly stopped them coming. Then change it.
