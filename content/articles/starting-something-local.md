Many of the best community activities start with one person saying "I'd go to
that if it existed." If that's you, here are the questions worth answering
before the first session.

## What's the shared activity?

People find it easier to meet over something than over nothing. Choose an
activity that gives everyone something to do, and that a beginner can join.

## Who is it for, and who might it leave out?

Think about time of day, cost, location, physical access and whether the
activity assumes experience. A Saturday morning walk suits some people and
excludes others. That's fine, as long as you've chosen it on purpose.

## Where will it happen?

Public and semi-public spaces — libraries, community centres, cafés, parks —
feel safer for first meetings than private homes. Check access, toilets and
what happens if it rains.

## How will people find out?

Word of mouth works best, but it needs a starting point: a noticeboard, a
local newsletter, a library, a GP surgery's community board.

## What happens if something goes wrong?

Think about first aid, how people will get in touch if you cancel, and what
you'll do if someone behaves badly. If the activity involves children or
adults who may be at risk, you will need proper safeguarding arrangements
before you start.

## Where we come in

If you have an idea, [tell us about it](/suggest/). We read every suggestion
and use them to decide what to run. We can't promise to take every idea
forward, but the ones that come up again and again are the ones we prioritise.
